# music2 0.2.0 (alpha)

**In short**
- The second download of music2. It is still an alpha: an early test version. Things will break, so keep a copy of any song you care about.
- New since 0.1.0: drum kits and a beat grid, a wavetable synth, mixing tools, sidechain by clicking, recording with takes and comping, Tune and editing a voice's notes, Bounce to audio, ⌘F to find any sound or sample, packs, and cards that draw pictures of their sound.
- A song made with 0.1.0 needs one step to play in 0.2.0: **Upgrade it** in the app, or `m2 upgrade` (see "Upgrading songs from 0.1.0" below).
- It runs on Macs with Apple silicon (M1 or newer), and on Linux PCs with an x86_64 processor. The Mac app still isn't signed by Apple: the first time you open it, click **Open Anyway** once (see "The first time on a Mac").

## What music2 is

music2 is a music studio: a DAW (digital audio workstation), the kind of app people make songs in, like GarageBand. It is made for people new to making music.

In music2 a song is a folder, and the folder is a small program. Code says how the song sounds (the instruments, the effects and the mix). Data files say what plays (the notes, the knob moves and the audio). The window shows the song as a timeline, a piano roll, cards with knobs, and a code editor. Every change you make there is saved into the files at once.

There is no AI inside the app. Scripts and AI agents can work on a song from outside, with the `m2` command line, and you see each change they make land in the window.

## What's new since 0.1.0

### Sounds and instruments

- **music2's sounds are packs now.** They come with the app: `@m2/synths` (the synths and FM sounds), `@m2/wavetable`, `@m2/drums`, `@m2/effects`, `@m2/mixing` and `@m2/vocals`. std, the part every song starts from, is now a small toolbox the packs are built from. Each song remembers which version of each pack it was made with ([Packs](https://breadbowlai.github.io/music2/packs.html)).
- **A wavetable synth** (`@m2/wavetable`), the kind most producers use today: two oscillators, each playing a table of waveforms you slide through, a filter with four shapes, two envelopes, an LFO and unison. Drop your own WAV on an oscillator to play it as a table. Presets: Slow pad, Round bass, Sharp lead and Short pluck.
- **A wave shape menu** on the synths (saw, square, triangle or sine), on every one but the 808.
- **The Sampler plays real recorded instruments**: regions, velocity layers (a softer recording for a softer note), round robin (a new recording each time a note repeats), release sounds and loops.
- **Instruments as data**, with no code: a pack can describe a recorded instrument in a file, and `m2 pack import-sfz` turns an SFZ instrument (a common free format) into a pack.
- **The sustain pedal** works when you play live.

### Drums

- **The Drum**: one drum on one track, picked from a menu of 17 sounds (kick, snare, clap, hats, open hat, toms, rim, cowbell, cymbal, shaker and more).
- **Drum kits**: **+ Track ▸ Drum kit (several tracks)** adds an 808 kit, a 909 kit or a Basic kit. Each drum gets its own track, fader and effects, and all go to one **Drums** bus, the kit's own fader.
- **The beat grid** (the **Beat** view, ⌥5): write a beat by clicking boxes, one row a drum, with a velocity lane and **+ Drum**.
- **Cut by**: a closed hat stops an open one that is still ringing, picked on the drum's card.
- Pads and keyboards play a kit, each pad its drum. A MIDI file's drums come in as a kit.
- The old one-track drum machine still plays. **Make it a kit** in its track's menu turns it into a kit when you choose.

### Finding sounds and samples

- **⌘F** (Ctrl+F on Linux): one search over instruments, sounds (presets), effects, samples and your plugins. **Space** plays a preview, **Enter** adds it ([Finding sounds](https://breadbowlai.github.io/music2/finding-sounds.html)).
- **Samples**: one-shots and loops from music2's own small drum kit, from packs, and from folders of your own. A loop at another tempo plays in time with your song, and the file is copied into the song with a note of where it came from ([Samples](https://breadbowlai.github.io/music2/samples.html)).
- **Packs**: install a pack of sounds from a folder (`m2 pack install`), or make your own ([Making a pack](https://breadbowlai.github.io/music2/making-a-pack.html)).
- **FLAC** files come in wherever WAV files do.
- Getting packs from an online library is built (⌘F's **From the library**, **Get**, `m2 pack get`), but the library itself isn't open yet: it shows only once you set its address ([Getting packs from the library](https://breadbowlai.github.io/music2/library.html)).

### Recording and vocals

- **Recording, the full set**: arm several tracks at once, each from its own input (inputs 3 and 4 too), punch in and out, a take each time round a loop, and overdub.
- **Comping**: **Takes** on a track opens a row for each take; swipe along one to use it for those bars, and build one take from the best parts of several.
- **Tune** (`@m2/vocals`): pitch correction. It moves each sung note onto the song's key, gently (Natural) or as the hard "robot" pop sound (Robot).
- **Edit a voice's notes**: right-click a vocal clip, then **Find notes**. Each sung note shows in the piano roll over its waveform: drag one up or down to fix its pitch, sideways to move it onto the beat, or **Straighten** its wobble. Only the notes you edit change.
- **Bounce to audio**: a track's menu turns it into an audio track of itself, to chop, reverse or pitch. The song sounds the same.

### Writing notes and arranging

- **More from the song's key** (**Key** in the top bar): the modes, the pentatonics and Blues beside Major and Minor; **Lock to key** in the piano roll, and **Chord ▾**, which stamps the key's chords. The key is part of the song now, so sounds hear it: Tune moves a voice onto it.
- **Grooves** in the note menu: Laid back, Pushed and Accents, beside Swing and Humanize.
- **Sections**: drag across the ruler, then copy, cut, paste, duplicate, insert or delete those bars on every track at once. Copy a chorus in one step ([Sections](https://breadbowlai.github.io/music2/sections.html)).

### Mixing and effects

- **`@m2/mixing`**: an eight-band EQ, a gate, a transient shaper, a de-esser, a multiband compressor, and **Pump**, which dips the sound on every beat by itself. There are 24 effects now, in seven groups (17 in 0.1.0).
- **Sidechain by clicking**: a Duck, a compressor or a gate has **Listens to** on its card. Pick the Kick, and the bass dips on every kick ([Sidechain](https://breadbowlai.github.io/music2/sounds.html#sidechain)).
- **Meters after the fader**: each strip's meter goes down with its fader. Above Master's fader: the song's loudness (LUFS, as streaming services measure it) and its peak.

### The cards and their pictures

- **The Device view shows a chain of cards**, one for each sound, left to right as the sound flows. Each card has a face (the knobs that matter most), tabs for the rest, and three sizes: Fold, Normal and Expand. **Patch** still shows the boxes and cables.
- **Menus, switches and number boxes** on the cards, for choices such as a wave's shape or a drum's sound, with labels in words.
- **Pictures you can drag**: an envelope, a filter's curve, one note as the sound plays it, the wavetable's table, the FM synth's partials, the limiter's ceiling, a sampler's keys, a scope. Drag a picture's dots to turn its knobs, as one undo step.
- **The EQ draws its whole curve**, measured from its sound, over the sound's spectrum while the song plays. Every card of std and music2's own packs has its picture, and a pack can draw pictures of its own.

### Saving and undo

- **Undo works after you close the song**, and code you typed but didn't save is offered back after a crash.
- The guide now says how to keep versions of your song with git ([Saving](https://breadbowlai.github.io/music2/saving.html)).

### Agents and `m2`

- New commands: `m2 upgrade`, `m2 add-kit`, `m2 make-kit`, `m2 add-sample`, `m2 search`, `m2 sections`, `m2 pack` (new, check, build, install, list, remove, lock, update, get, import-sfz) and `m2 bounce --in-place`. Every one is in [Commands](https://breadbowlai.github.io/music2/commands.html).
- `m2` refuses a word a command doesn't take, rather than skip it.
- Agents can look at a sound while the song plays (its wave, its spectrum, its levels and its meters) and read a card's picture as numbers, through the local API ([The local API](https://breadbowlai.github.io/music2/api.html)).
- A gesture you make right after your own edit now waits for that edit and lands, and never on the wrong clip or note.

### Fixes and safety

- A pack's sound code has a time limit: a pack that hangs is stopped, and you are told.
- Downloads go over https only, and each is checked by its size and hash.
- Exporting a song whose code never built is refused in words.
- The graph view no longer flickers when its scrollbars come and go.
- Security updates: wasmtime 50.0.1 (RUSTSEC-2026-0424) and DOMPurify 3.4.17.

## Upgrading songs from 0.1.0

Before 1.0, music2 keeps no backwards compatibility: a new version may change how a song is written, and an old song may need a step to play. That is a stated goal of the project ([goals](https://github.com/BreadBowlAI/music2/blob/main/docs/goals.md)), so music2 can still change for the better.

0.2.0 moved the sounds into packs, so a song made with 0.1.0 (it asks for `std = "0.4"`) opens, but stays silent until it is upgraded. The status pill in the top bar says **Made for an older music2**.

| To | Do this |
|---|---|
| Upgrade a song in the app | click the status pill, then **Upgrade it**. One Undo (⌘Z) takes it all back |
| See what would change first | `m2 upgrade <song> --check`: it writes nothing |
| Upgrade from the terminal | `m2 upgrade <song>`: it prints each line before (`-`) and after (`+`) |

- **What it changes**, and nothing else: the `@m2/core` and std lines in `music2.toml`, a line for each pack the song plays, `music2.lock` (the exact versions), the imports of sounds that moved into packs, and the old single drums, which become the Drum with their knobs converted. Not your notes, your mix or your comments.
- **The sound stays the same**, bit for bit, wherever it can. If something can't be converted exactly (a knob your code works out, say), nothing is written: it names each place by file and line, with the line to write. Change those by hand, then upgrade again.
- **The simple kick and hat change.** Songs made from 0.1.0's Starter beat and First song play the simple kick and hat (`kick` and `hat` from `std/instruments`), and the Resample example the simple kick. They become the Drum's kick and hats, which are other sounds, a drum machine's. The upgrade names them, by file and line, so you can listen and turn their knobs.
- **A drum machine track** isn't changed, so it sounds exactly as before. **Make it a kit** (or `m2 make-kit`) turns it into a drum kit when you choose: the same drums, pitches and levels, though each hit's noise comes out a little different.
- **Your presets** for the old single drums aren't changed: the upgrade names them, so you can save them again.

How it works: [Opening a song made with an older music2](https://breadbowlai.github.io/music2/songs.html#opening-a-song-made-with-an-older-music2).

## Install on a Mac

1. Download `music2-0.2.0-macos-arm64.zip`. Double-click it, if your browser hasn't unpacked it already. You get `music2.app`.
2. Drag `music2.app` into your **Applications** folder. If 0.1.0 is there, replace it.

### The first time on a Mac

macOS checks every app from the internet. This one isn't signed with an Apple developer certificate, so macOS stops it the first time. You allow it once. These are the steps on macOS 15 (Sequoia) and newer:

1. Open music2. macOS says "Apple could not verify music2 is free of malware". Click **Done**. (Don't click Move to Trash.)
2. Open **System Settings**, then **Privacy & Security**.
3. Scroll down to **Security**. It says "music2 was blocked to protect your Mac". Click **Open Anyway**.
4. Type your password (or use Touch ID).
5. Open music2 again. Click **Open Anyway** once more.

On macOS 11 to 14: Control-click music2 in Applications, choose **Open**, then click **Open** in the message. If it still won't open, allow it in **System Settings → Privacy & Security** (on macOS 11 and 12: **System Preferences → Security & Privacy → General**) with **Open Anyway**.

From then on music2 opens like any other app. Signing the app with Apple, so this step goes away, may come in a later release.

**No Open Anyway button?** Newer macOS versions sometimes don't show it. Then open the **Terminal** app, paste this line, and press Return. It removes the "downloaded from the internet" mark from music2 only, and opens it:

```
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/music2.app && open /Applications/music2.app
```

`m2`, the command line, is inside the app: `/Applications/music2.app/Contents/MacOS/m2`.

## Install on Linux

### The .deb (Ubuntu 22.04 or newer, Debian 12 or newer)

```bash
sudo apt install ./music2_0.2.0_amd64.deb
```

apt also installs the system packages music2 needs, if they aren't there yet: `libwebkit2gtk-4.1-0` (the window's web view), `libgtk-3-0`, `libasound2` (sound, through ALSA), `libdbus-1-3`, and glibc 2.35 or newer (`libc6`). It was tried on Ubuntu 24.04 only: on 22.04 and Debian 12 it should run, as those have every package it needs, but no one has opened it there yet. Installed over 0.1.0, it replaces it.

Then open music2 from your apps, or type `music2` in a terminal. `m2` is installed as `/usr/bin/m2`. To remove music2: `sudo apt remove music2`.

### The AppImage (other Linux)

The AppImage carries most of its libraries. Your system needs glibc 2.38 or newer, ALSA's library (`libasound2`), and FUSE's `fusermount` program, which desktop Ubuntu and Debian have (the `fuse3` package). It doesn't need FUSE 2 (`libfuse2`).

```bash
chmod +x music2-0.2.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage
./music2-0.2.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage
```

Without `fusermount`, add `--appimage-extract-and-run` at the end of the second line.

### m2 for the AppImage

The AppImage can't hand `m2` to your shell. Download `m2-0.2.0-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` too, then:

```bash
tar xzf m2-0.2.0-linux-x86_64.tar.gz
./m2-0.2.0-linux-x86_64/bin/m2 --version
```

Keep its `bin` and `lib` folders together: `m2` finds the standard sounds, music2's own packs, the templates and the examples in `lib`. It needs glibc 2.35 or newer and ALSA's library (`libasound2`, or `libasound2t64` on Ubuntu 24.04 and newer), even on a computer that plays no sound.

## Check your download

The `SHA256SUMS` file holds a checksum of every file. Put it in the folder you downloaded to, and run:

- Linux: `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing`
- Mac: `shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing`

Each file you downloaded should say `OK`.

Each file also carries a signed provenance: a record, signed by GitHub, that music2's own release workflow built it from this release's commit. With the [GitHub CLI](https://cli.github.com) (`gh`):

```sh
gh attestation verify music2_0.2.0_amd64.deb --repo BreadBowlAI/music2 \
  --signer-workflow BreadBowlAI/music2/.github/workflows/release.yml --source-ref refs/tags/v0.2.0
```

Use the name of the file you downloaded. It should say `✓ Verification succeeded!`. If it says `Error`, don't open the file.

## Known gaps

This is an alpha. The full list is [What music2 can't do yet](https://github.com/BreadBowlAI/music2/blob/main/docs/gaps.md); each gap keeps its number there. The biggest a producer would notice:

| # | What's missing |
|---|---|
| 62 | Sounds that make notes: an arpeggiator, chords from one key, note repeat |
| 52 | Slicing a vocal or a loop at its hits, onto keys or pads |
| 56 | MIDI learn: tying a knob on your controller to one in the app |
| 57 | Macro knobs: one knob that turns several at once |
| 14 | Automation curves between points (today: straight lines) |
| 24 | Recording the pitch and mod wheels and the sustain pedal into a clip |
| 36 | Export as MP3 or AAC, or of a range only |
| 18 | MP3 and AIFF files (WAV and FLAC only), and warp markers placed by hand |
| 55 | A flanger, a convolution reverb and granular effects |
| 60 | A reference track to compare your mix with |
| 39 | The online library of packs isn't open yet |
| 43 | The Mac app isn't signed or notarized by Apple (the one-time step above) |
| 45 | No automatic updates: download each new release yourself |
| 44 | No Intel Macs, no Windows, no Linux on ARM |
| 33 | Plugins are CLAP only: no VST3 or Audio Units (not planned) |

On Linux, only Ubuntu 24.04 is checked by hand. The licences of the code inside music2 that others wrote are in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt`: in `music2.app/Contents/Resources`, in `/usr/share/doc/music2` from the `.deb`, and beside `bin` in m2's `.tar.gz`.

## Report a bug

Open an issue: <https://github.com/BreadBowlAI/music2/issues/new/choose>. Please say:

- which file you installed, and your system (for example "the .deb on Ubuntu 24.04", or "the Mac app on macOS 15, M2"),
- what you did, what you expected, and what happened,
- if you can, the song folder, zipped, or a screenshot.

`m2 --version` prints the version you have.

A security problem? Please don't open a public issue: report it in private, as [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/BreadBowlAI/music2/blob/main/SECURITY.md) says.
